Buffing Profile and Crown Radius: Getting the Casing Right Before the Tread Goes On

Buffing is not just removing old tread. TIA's recommended practice lists three goals: size the casing to the tread, shape the crown to the right radius, and prepare the surface. What the published guidance says, and what it means for an in-house line.

Published October 17, 2026 · Good Hope Retreaders

When a casing comes off the inspection line, the next job decides whether the tread you apply will sit properly: buffing. It is easy to think of it as taking the old tread off. The published practice treats it as three separate jobs, and a shop that runs its own line is the one that has to get all three right, every time, because no outside retreader is doing it for you.

This article sticks to what the trade guidance we could find actually says, flags where a figure applies only to one tire type, and labels our own reasoning as reasoning. It is a planning summary, not a process specification.

What buffing is supposed to achieve

The Tire Industry Association’s recommended practice RP-01-01, “Tire Profiling for Retreading” describes three buffing goals, as summarized from the document:

  • Size the casing to the width of the precured tread, or to the mold.
  • Shape the crown to the correct radius.
  • Prepare the surface for adhesion.

The same practice says the radius chosen has to allow all of the existing tread-design grooves to be removed, without damaging the tire. For precured systems it says truck tires must be buffed while inflated. Its companion glossary (RP-01/02-10) defines the buffed radius as the curvature of the buffed surface measured from shoulder to shoulder.

The shoulder is where it can go wrong

RP-01-01 also addresses deep shoulder designs: they should be buffed and trimmed so that any remaining void does not extend more than 1/4 inch (6 mm) under the edge of the tread, on each side. In plain terms, the shoulder trim and the tread width have to be matched, because an unsupported tread edge is where trouble starts. That is an interpretation of the figure, not a quote from the practice.

A worked example: wide-base casings

TRIB’s article on retreading wide-base tires gives concrete setup figures for that tire type: an expandable rim 14.5 inches wide, a minimum inflation of 18 to 22 psi before buffing starts, and a target buff radius of 67 inches, plus or minus 2 inches, with a 67-inch template available from a TRIB member. These numbers are specific to wide-base tires and should not be applied to standard dual-size casings. Our guide to wide-base single tires covers the fleet side of that decision.

Texture still matters

Profile is the shape; texture is the finish. The TRMG buffing-texture practice (RP-01/02-23, BTS6) rates six textures, with BT3 and BT4 the preferred outside textures for retreads, and BT6 too coarse for repairs or retreads. We cover that standard in our guide to buffing, rasping and bond quality.

What this means for an in-house line

This section is our reasoning, not sourced data. Three practical points follow from the published guidance:

  • Set the profile per casing family. Radius and width are not one-size settings. Keep a written profile for each casing size you run and the tread it takes, and check it against the tread manufacturer’s own instructions.
  • Make the setup repeatable. Rim width, inflation and template are all variables the published guidance names. A shop that fixes them in a procedure is less dependent on operator feel.
  • Record it. Note the profile used against the casing’s record, so a later bond or edge problem can be traced. This ties into the casing salvage-rate KPI.

The equipment side is straightforward to spec: a buffing machine with the right radius-faced rasps, and expanding rims that fit the casings you run, supplied with local Ontario service.

Source note: retread.org, tireindustry.org and Tire Review pages could not be opened directly from our publishing environment, so the points above reflect the documents as returned and summarized by search. We did not retrieve the full text of RP-01-01, so we are not stating its radius tables. Confirm each figure against the cited document before relying on it.
