# Winter Tire and Chain Rules for Canadian Fleets: What BC and Quebec Actually Require

Pillar: B (economics/authority — regulatory)
Published: 2026-09-09

## Angle
Canada-specific regulatory content fitting Good Hope's Ontario/Canadian positioning.
Busts a common assumption (Quebec's mandate actually exempts heavy trucks; BC's targets
them directly) and answers a genuine practical question for fleets — can a retread carry
a 3PMSF winter rating.

## Sources used (found via web search this run)
- Gouvernement du Québec, official winter tire requirements page (quebec.ca): mandate
  period Dec 1–Mar 15 inclusive; heavy vehicles ≥4,500 kg and trailers exempt; vehicles
  registered outside Quebec exempt; fines $200–$300.
- Government of British Columbia, commercial vehicle tire and chain requirements
  (gov.bc.ca): Oct 1–Apr 30 season; vehicles 5,000–11,794 kg LGVW must carry chains
  unless equipped with 3PMSF or M+S tires; vehicles ≥11,794 kg LGVW must carry steel
  chains on major highways regardless; named routes (Coquihalla, Hwy 1 Kicking
  Horse/Rogers Pass, Hwy 3, Hwy 97C); fines $121–$598.
- USTMA (U.S. Tire Manufacturers Association) — TISB 50, "USTMA Definition for Truck and
  Bus Tires for Use in Severe Snow Conditions" (ustires.org): Snow Grip Index ≥1.10
  against the ASTM E1136 reference tire under the ASTM F1805 snow traction test.
  3PMSF symbol introduced 1999 by USTMA and the Tire and Rubber Association of Canada
  (TRAC) — widely corroborated across multiple industry sources.
- Goodyear Commercial Tire & Service Centers (goodyearctsc.com) retread product listing:
  BFGoodrich Route Control D meets USTMA snow traction / TRAC requirements when
  retreaded through an authorized dealer — used as the concrete example that a retread
  can carry 3PMSF when built with a qualifying tread pattern.

## Guardrails followed
- No fabricated fleet example, case study, or figure — every number traces to the
  government or industry-association source above.
- Careful to frame 3PMSF certification as tread-pattern-specific (new or retread), not a
  blanket claim that "retreads are winter rated" or that they aren't.
- Good Hope framed accurately as equipment/consumables supplier, not a certifier of tire
  patterns or a retread operator running shops for customers.
