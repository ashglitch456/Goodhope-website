# Buffing Dust: Why OSHA and NFPA Treat It as a Combustible Hazard, Not Just Housekeeping

Pillar: A — Product/equipment education (shop safety angle, tied to the dust-collector-cyclone-filter product)
Published: 2026-09-04

## Angle
Buffing throws off fine reclaimed rubber dust as a routine byproduct. OSHA's own
combustible-dust guidance names tire-buffing rubber dust directly as a hazard source —
this isn't a hypothetical or a stretch. The post explains the regulatory framework
(OSHA SHIB + NFPA 654 + the Combustible Dust NEP) and translates it into practical
shop controls, with Good Hope's cyclone dust collector as the natural product tie-in.

Deliberately NOT covered: any claim that buffing dust carries IARC-classified cancer
risk. That classification applies to the broader rubber-manufacturing industry
(curing chemicals, N-nitrosamines, fumes) — conflating it with mechanical buffing dust
in a retread shop would be inaccurate and overreaching. Kept the health angle to
general dust/ventilation housekeeping only.

## Sources (checked live via web search, 2026-09-04)
1. **OSHA SHIB 07-31-2005 (updated 11-12-2014), "Combustible Dust in Industry:
   Preventing and Mitigating the Effects of Fire and Explosions."**
   https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/SHIB073105.pdf
   Names "reclaimed tire tread rubber (rubber dust) from buffing the tread area of a
   worn tire to texture the surface for recapping" directly as a combustible-dust
   source, alongside wood/food/metal/plastics dust.
2. **OSHA Combustible Dust National Emphasis Program, Directive CPL 03-00-008
   (reissued).** https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/enforcement/directives/CPL_03-00-008.pdf
   Confirms the active enforcement/inspection posture for facilities generating or
   handling combustible dust.
3. **NFPA 654 — Standard for the Prevention of Fire and Dust Explosions from the
   Manufacturing, Processing, and Handling of Combustible Particulate Solids.**
   Referenced via OSHA guidance and secondary industry coverage
   (https://ohsonline.com/articles/2024/05/19/ensure-the-safety-of-dust-collectors-to-reduce-explosion-risk.aspx,
   https://camfilapc.com/blog/understanding-osha-and-nfpa-regulations-for-metal-dust-collection/)
   as the recognized standard OSHA points to under the General Duty Clause.
4. **"Over half of combustible dust explosions occur within dust collectors."**
   https://ohsonline.com/articles/2024/05/19/ensure-the-safety-of-dust-collectors-to-reduce-explosion-risk.aspx
   Used to explain why collectors need explosion relief/suppression, not just filtration.
5. **dustsafetyscience.com, "The Rubber Industry and Combustible Dust Explosions and
   Hazards"** — https://dustsafetyscience.com/combustible-dust-rubber-industry/
   Corroborates that buffing is one of the operations in the rubber/retread space that
   most often generates combustible dust.
6. **OSHA Publication 3371, "Hazard Communication Guidance for Combustible Dusts."**
   https://www.osha.gov/publications/3371combustible-dust — general framework citation.
