# Why More Fleets Are Switching to Retreads: The Cost & Circular Economy Case in 2026

On July 16, 2026, the Tire Industry Association (TIA) and the Tire Retread & Repair Information Bureau (TRIB) led a delegation of retread industry leaders to Capitol Hill to push for a bipartisan federal bill built specifically to expand retread adoption. That's not a routine trade-group visit — it's a signal that the economics and the environmental case for retreading have gotten strong enough to justify a federal push. Here's what's actually driving fleets toward retreads right now, with the real numbers behind it.

## The news hook: a federal push behind retreading

The bill in question — the Retreaded Tire Jobs, Supply Chain Security, and Sustainability Act (S.2790 in the Senate, H.R.3401 in the House) — would create a federal procurement preference for retreaded tires across public-sector fleets and establish a tax credit of up to $30 per retreaded tire, or 30% of the purchase price, for both public and private fleet buyers. TIA and TRIB's pitch to lawmakers was straightforward: retreading already saves fleets close to $3 billion a year, and federal policy could accelerate adoption further, particularly among fleets that haven't made the switch yet.

Whether or not the bill passes, its existence tells you something the industry already knows from its own purchasing data: retreading isn't a cost-cutting afterthought anymore — it's an income-statement line item serious fleets manage on purpose.

## The market is already growing — the real numbers

Independent market research backs up the trend, even if the growth-rate estimates vary by firm and methodology:

- **TechSci Research** projects the global retread tire market growing from USD 11.53 billion in 2025 to USD 14.83 billion by 2031 — a 4.28% CAGR.
- **Future Market Insights** puts the 2026 global market at roughly USD 11.1 billion, with a more modest 2.8% CAGR forecast through 2036.

The two firms don't agree on the exact growth rate, and we're not going to pretend they do. What both agree on is the direction: steady, multi-year growth in a market that was already worth well over $10 billion heading into 2026.

## The circular economy case — what retreading actually saves

The sustainability argument for retreading isn't a marketing add-on; it's arithmetic about what a casing already contains. A commercial truck tire's casing — the structural carcass beneath the tread — makes up roughly 75% of the tire's total mass, according to a March 2026 industry analysis from Scrap Tire News. When the tread wears out, that casing is typically still structurally sound, holding most of the tire's embodied material and energy intact.

That's why the per-tire numbers TIA and TRIB brought to their 2026 Capitol Hill advocacy are the ones that matter for the circular-economy case: producing a retread requires roughly 15 fewer gallons of oil and 90 to 100 fewer pounds of raw material than manufacturing a new commercial truck tire. Multiply that across an industry, and TRIB's own published figures put the U.S./Canada retread industry's annual impact at an estimated 217.5 million gallons of oil saved and 1.4 billion pounds of landfill avoidance.

For a fleet under pressure to report on sustainability metrics — not just cost — that's a real, defensible number to put in front of a customer or a board, not a vague "we care about the planet" line.

## How this connects to fleet cost-per-mile

None of this works as a pitch if the tires don't perform, which is why the cost and the sustainability stories run together rather than separately. We've broken down the purchase-price gap and the cost-per-mile math in detail in [Retreads vs. New Tires: What the Cost-Per-Mile Numbers Actually Show](retreads-vs-new-tires-cost-per-mile.html), and the safety data fleets ask about is covered in [Retread Tire Safety: What the TRIB/TIA Data Actually Shows](retread-tire-safety-trib-tia-data.html). The 2026 trend is really those two arguments converging with a third: fleets increasingly have to answer for their environmental footprint too, and retreading is one of the few tire decisions that improves the cost line and the sustainability line at the same time — provided the casing was sound to begin with, which is why [casing inspection](casing-inspection-5-point-check.html) still matters more than any other single step in the process.
