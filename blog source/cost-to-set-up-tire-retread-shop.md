# How Much Does It Cost to Set Up a Tire Retread Shop in Canada & the USA?

If you're weighing whether to open a retread shop — or add a retread line to an existing tire business — the first question is always the same: what is this actually going to cost me?

It's a surprisingly hard question to get a straight answer to. Most equipment suppliers hide their pricing behind a "request a quote" form, and most of the "how to start a retreading business" guides online are written for other markets, quoting numbers in currencies and cost structures that mean nothing to a shop in Ontario or Ohio.

So here's the straight answer, in Canadian dollars, all-in, for the North American market — no quote wall, no guesswork.

## The short answer

A small, precure retread shop — the kind that handles commercial truck casings for local fleets — can be equipped for roughly $45,000 to $62,000 CAD for the machinery, tooling, and an opening stock of consumables, before facility, labour, and permits. A fuller build with higher-capacity or automated equipment scales up from there.

That's a range and not a single number because it depends on how many tires you plan to run and how much of the process you automate. Below, we break it down piece by piece so you can build a figure that fits your shop — and everything is priced all-in to your facility, delivered from our Ontario warehouse.

## Why the math works: retreading saves 30–50% per tire

Before the costs, the reason people do this: retreading a commercial truck casing typically costs a fleet 30–50% less than buying a comparable new tire, and a good casing can be retreaded multiple times over its life. That's why roughly 80% of major North American fleets retread their drive and trailer axles. For a shop, that cost advantage is what turns equipment into a business — every casing you retread is a margin a fleet can't get from new tires. The equipment is the up-front cost; the retreads are the return.

## 1. The core machinery

These are the machines that make up the retread line itself. A minimal precure setup needs a way to inspect the casing, remove the old tread, apply new tread, and cure it. A realistic starter line includes:

- Inspection spreader — opens the casing so you can inspect for injuries before investing labour in it.

- Buffing machine — removes the worn tread and trues the casing to the correct diameter and radius.

- Tread builder — applies cushion gum and the new precured tread to the prepared casing.

- Envelope / sealing station — seals the built tire in an envelope for the cure.

- Electric curing chamber — bonds the new tread to the casing with controlled heat and pressure.

- Dust collector — captures buffing dust for a clean, compliant shop.

A complete core line of these machines typically runs in the region of $30,000–$45,000 CAD, depending on capacity and automation level.

Two things worth knowing. First, electric curing chambers — the kind we supply — use electric heating elements instead of a steam boiler, which means lower energy costs, no boiler licence or fuel line, and a smaller footprint. For a small shop, that's a meaningful saving on both setup and operating cost. Second, capacity scales cleanly: a 4-tire chamber suits a starter shop, and you can step up to 8-tire and larger as your volume grows without changing the rest of the line.

## 2. Components and tooling

The machines need the right hardware to hold and process the tire sizes you run:

| Item | All-in CAD |

|---|---|

| Expandable hubs (per size) | $650 – $850 |

| Expanding rims (per size) | $850 – $1,150 |

| Curing flanges / rims | $150 – $300 |

| Extruder gun (injury repair) | $1,500 – $2,000 |

| Repair tool kit — rasps, cutters, stones | $1,500 – $2,500 |

You'll need hubs and rims in each tire size range you plan to service, so this scales with the breadth of casings you take on. Most starter shops begin with the one or two most common commercial sizes and expand their range as demand grows.

## 3. Your first inventory of consumables

Consumables are the ongoing cost of doing business — the materials consumed on every retread. A sensible opening stock includes:

- Precured tread rubber — the new tread itself, in the patterns and widths your customers need.

- Cushion (bonding) gum — the layer that bonds new tread to casing.

- Bonding cement — for repairs and surface preparation.

- Curing envelopes and sealing rings — reusable across many cures.

An opening inventory of consumables for a small shop runs roughly $6,000–$10,000 CAD. Unlike your machines, this is a recurring reorder — which is exactly why it pays to source from a supplier who stocks locally and ships fast, so you're never idle waiting on materials.

## 4. The costs that aren't equipment

These vary enormously by location, but leaving them out would give you a false picture:

- Facility — space for the line, casing storage, and finished inventory, with adequate power. Industrial space in Windsor rents very differently than in Toronto or Los Angeles, so localize this.

- Electrical setup — budget for an electrician to run the right service to your machines. New shop owners routinely underestimate this.

- Labour — a small precure line can be run by a small team; a buffer operator and a builder are the core skilled roles.

- Insurance and permits — general business insurance plus any provincial or state environmental permits for a rubber-processing operation.

We won't invent numbers for these — they depend on your city, your lease, and your local wage market. But budget for them deliberately; they're real.

## 5. Certification and compliance

Retreaded tires are safety-critical, so quality standards matter — for liability and for winning fleet accounts. In North America that typically means building to DOT requirements for retreaded tires, and many successful shops pursue TIA (Tire Industry Association) training and certification, which lends real credibility with fleet buyers. Requirements vary by jurisdiction and tire type, so confirm the specifics with your provincial or state authority and the relevant industry association before you commit — treat this as a starting checklist, not legal advice.

## 6. Putting it together

For a small precure shop running common commercial truck sizes, here's the shape of the investment:

| Category | All-in CAD |

|---|---|

| Core machinery | $30,000 – $45,000 |

| Components & tooling (starter size range) | $7,000 – $9,000 |

| Opening consumables inventory | $6,000 – $10,000 |

| Facility, electrical, labour, permits | Varies by location — budget separately |

The headline: the equipment to start retreading is well within reach for an independent operator — a fraction of what most people assume, and a fraction of what a comparable new-tire retail operation ties up in inventory. And because retreads carry a 30–50% cost advantage over new tires, a shop that keeps its line busy recovers that equipment investment out of ongoing retread margin.
